# Paid-game quality review

The user is building games to sell. Passing tests and uploading the latest build
are necessary but do not establish that the product is enjoyable or well presented.
Use this reference when preparing a player-facing release, store assets, or a
significant player experience change. Do not turn minor maintenance into a full audit.

- Reuse established audience, business model, visual direction and budget. Ask for
  missing decisions only when they change the work; do not make the user repeatedly
  explain that quality matters. Do not promise sales without market evidence.
- State the player benefit and strongest current reason to play before making assets.
  Inspect the actual game at representative early/middle/later moments. Distinguish
  verified behavior, editorial judgment, and feedback still needed from real players.
- Review separately: technical correctness; player experience (clarity, challenge,
  choice, pacing); commercial presentation (clear genre, compelling action, truthful
  screenshots and consistent art). A technical pass does not approve the other two.
- Store screenshots must show what the player does. For an action game, lead with
  active combat and a readable protagonist/opponent relationship; include a real Boss
  encounter when Bosses are a selling point. Menus and secondary systems come later.
  For other genres choose their actual core interaction, not combat by default.
- Capture multiple candidates and inspect the chosen files at full size AND thumbnail
  scale. Reject empty fields, startup/tutorial notices, modal overlays, washed-out
  flashes, obscured protagonists/Bosses, redundant scenes and unreadable clutter.
  Do not inflate spectacle with invented enemies, altered combat rules, or AI-generated
  frames presented as gameplay. Record source version, setup and capture provenance.
- Review the final published order and images, not just successful upload responses.
  If existing gameplay cannot support compelling honest images, report the product
  weakness and propose a scoped improvement; do not hide it with misleading artwork.
- Keep a short selection rationale and unresolved limitations. Share an actual visual
  with the user for review without inventing an extra approval gate where publication
  is already authorized. Update persistent project instructions after explicit feedback.

Reference: [itch.io quality guidelines](https://itch.io/docs/creators/quality-guidelines)
require accurate representation that interests prospective players, recommend
screenshots, and prohibit misleading imagery. The editorial criteria above are the
studio's working standard, not a claim that the platform guarantees conversion.
